Chapter One

Casual Mornings

The family of the Samos were the humblest and most generous family in all of Hokkaido. Tadashi Samo owned a tea stall, which never got more than enough customers to feed themselves, yet they would rather go hungry than not feed a guest.

Tadashi Samo woke at 7 a.m. on a Tuesday. An hour later, he was up and ready at his tea stall. It was just like how Tadashi had inherited it from his father, his grandfather, and his great-great-grandfather.

A dirt road in the front, never-ending green meadows in the open, and just his and his wife's house in the distance.

He was nostalgic for his childhood and worried that no customers might buy anything, until he noticed something peculiar.

Four people were walking down the dirt path. Worst of all, they were different colors: Red, Blue, Green, and Yellow.

"Maybe, this isn't my average morning," thought Tadashi.

"How much for four cups?" asked the Red one.

"20," they both said simultaneously.

Now, Tadashi was scared out of his wits.

First, they were colored. Now, they're reading my mind. Tadashi thought.

"You've got to stop scaring people, Red," said Blue.

"Ah, it's not even fun anymore," Red replied.

"Do you want four cups of tea, sir?" asked Tadashi, who was getting more and more impatient.

"Yeah, whatever," said Yellow, and tossed some coins. "Keep the change."

"What's the time and place again?" asked Green.

"It's the 7th of May, 1010, and we're in Hokkaido, Japan, sir," Tadashi replied.

"Have you seen Purple anywhere yet, Green?" Red asked.

"No, not at all," Green replied.

Purple? More? Tadashi wondered.

Tadashi was now getting more and more interested by these colored people. All his life, he had only seen grey, or what he thought, non-colored people. Seeing different colored people was piquing his curiosity.

Tadashi saw a purplish object zooming across the sky, or so he thought.

As Tadashi was about to speak, three golden nets dropped out of nowhere and trapped Blue, Green, and Yellow.

"Wha—?" said Red.

A purple-colored person floated down.

"YOU!" shouted Red, and tried to punch him, but Purple dashed away.

"You're good, BUT NOT GOOD ENOUGH! Without them, you're just useless," Purple said.

But Red seemed unfazed.

Then, Purple started punching Red, but Red failed to block it. Then Purple grabbed Red by the neck, zoomed into the horizon, only to come back down and throw Red against the ground.

Red was severely injured and bruised. You could tell he was almost dead.

Not my normal morning, Tadashi told himself.

"Giving up so easily? You aren't like you used to be," said Purple, and was about to finish him off with a blow.

But Red rolled to the side and got up.

"Sacrificial Exchange: Red, Blue, Green, and Yellow for the Holy Seal of One Thousand and Sixteen Years used upon Purple, and reincarnation of Red, Blue, Green, and Yellow in one thousand years."

"WAIT! NO!" yelled Purple and desperately tried to attack Red, but it was too late.

An orange band of fire with ancient Japanese symbols wrapped around Purple, instantly freezing him on the spot and making a bright orange sphere.

Then it shrank to the size of a tennis ball and turned Purple.

Red, Blue, Green, and Yellow dissipated into thin air right in front of Tadashi.

Tadashi pinched himself to check if it was a dream or not, but unfortunately, it wasn't.

After one thousand and fifteen years, in Tokyo, a boy named Red woke up with a start.

Chapter Two

Back in Bed

It was a Monday.

"Ugh, I wish Mondays were holidays," groaned Red.

Red's parents always told him it was a rare skin disease that made him colored, but Red thought otherwise.

Still, it was the first day of school today, and he joined a new one, so he was kind of excited and nervous at the same time.

He ate some pancakes with maple syrup and got dressed. He slung his backpack over his shoulder, yelled "Bye," and ran off to the bus stop.

That's when he froze.

Sitting at the bus stop was a blue person, pretty much the same age as him.

"Hey," the blue guy said.

"Hello, I guess," replied Red.

"What's your name?"

"It's Blue. You?"

"Red."

Both of them got onto the bus, but Red had a strange feeling about Blue, like he had met him before or something.

Red brushed it off as déjà vu.

"So, why're you colored like I am?" Blue asked.

"Rare skin disease, apparently," Red said, though he wasn't really sure.

"Woah, same here!" Blue exclaimed as he sat down.

"So," Red started, "wanna be friends?"

"I thought we already were!" Blue beamed.

"Can I ask you something?" said Red.

"Yeah, sure. What is it?"

"I have a gut feeling that our skin color isn't actually a disease."

"I mean, I do too," Blue mumbled.

"Show me your timetable for a second."

"Oh, it's the same."

Red felt more strange energy.

"You felt that?" Blue said, looking out the window.

"Yeah, I did," Red replied.

"Do you have accidents like I do?"

"What? What accidents?"

"Never mind, don't worry about it."

"Come on, Red, tell me what it is!" Blue pestered.

"Fine," Red said. "Do you, like, accidentally light things on fire when you're angry or sad?"

"Hmm, no, not quite," Blue started. "I wet things when I get angry."

"What?" said Red, confused. "What do you mean, wet?"

"Like, I spawn water out of nowhere. I have no idea why."

The bus stopped.

"We'd better get to class," said Red. "We're pretty late."

Red and Blue slumped down onto their chairs. Red was still wondering about Blue and their similarities.

Red could feel everyone staring at him and Blue.

"Good morning, class!" the teacher said brightly.

"My name is Mrs. Storm, and I will be teaching English for you this year," she added and wrote her name on the board.

"We'll be introducing each other now," she said. "Why don't you start?"

She pointed at Red.

"Me? My name is Red."

"Okay, Red. Next, what about—"

"May I come in, uh, Mrs. Storm?" said someone at the door.

Red looked and his jaw dropped.

He was green and resembled Blue.

"Where can I sit?"

The green figure looked around before picking the seat next to Red.

"Let me guess, your name is Green?" Red asked.

"Yeah, it is. How did you know?" Green replied, slightly surprised.

Ping!

The bell rang. It was recess.

"Finally!" Red exclaimed.

"Everyone got their lunchboxes?" Blue asked.

"Yep!" said Red, Blue, and Green simultaneously.

"Then, let's dig in!"

Red got a chicken panini with ketchup packets, Blue had pizza rolls, and Green got himself a jar of Nutella.

"Hey, can I join you?" asked someone from behind.

Red turned around and his heart skipped a beat.

There he was: Yellow, just standing there.

How many colored people are there? Why have I never seen them before? Why are they appearing now?

Thoughts flooded his brain.

"Er, are you there?" Yellow said.

Red snapped back to reality.

"Yes. Yes, I am there."

Red rolled over and looked at the clock next to his bedside. 11:00 p.m.

It was getting late, but you couldn't blame him. He had a big day.

Does meeting four colored people and befriending them on first sight count as eventful? Red thought.

It's 11:00 p.m. What am I doing?

Red closed his eyes and drifted off into a deep slumber.

Strange Discoveries
